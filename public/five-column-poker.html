How to Play
Five-Column Chinese Poker · 1-Swap Variant

Heads-Up Mode

Two players. You're dealt 5 cards, one per column. The opponent gets the same. Each column will hold exactly 5 cards by the end.

The Swap (first draw only): On your first draw, instead of placing the drawn card normally, you may move one seed card from its column into a different column. The drawn card then fills the now-empty source column. Use it to consolidate paired or suited cards.

20 draw rounds follow. Each round you draw one card and place it. Placements lock — no further moves. When all slots fill, each of your columns is compared to the AI's same-numbered column. Win = +1, loss = −1, scoop (all 5) = +5 bonus.

Solo Mode (Poker Squares)

Classic 1908 solitaire variant. 25 cards dealt one at a time into a 5×5 grid. Once placed, cards lock. When the grid is full, you score 10 poker hands: the 5 rows AND the 5 columns. Sum all 10 hand scores to get your total.

Two published scoring systems:

American reflects poker hand probability — full houses, quads, and flushes carry the highest values. Target: 200 points.

English reflects difficulty of forming the hand within Poker Squares specifically — straights are rewarded heavily (+12, vs +5 for flushes). Target: 70 points.

Strategy note: the established opening pattern is to commit each row to a flush attempt while building straights/full houses/quads in the columns, with one row designated as the "junk" line for misfits.

Hand Rankings (both modes)

Standard poker: Royal Flush > Straight Flush > Quads > Full House > Flush > Straight > Three of a Kind > Two Pair > One Pair > High Card. The wheel (A-2-3-4-5) counts as a 5-high straight.
